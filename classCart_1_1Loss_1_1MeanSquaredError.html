Detailed Description

template<std::floating_point FloatType>
class Cart::Loss::MeanSquaredError< FloatType >

Mean squared error.

The MSE is defined as:

\begin{eqnarray*} \mathrm{MSE}(y, w) &=& \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^nw_i\left[y_i - \hat\pi(y, w)\right]^2 \\ &=& \frac{1}{W}\left[\sum_{i=1}^nw_iy_i^2 - 2\hat\pi(y, w)\sum_{i=1}^nw_iy_i\right] + \hat\pi(y, w)^2 \\ &=& \frac{1}{W}\left[\mathrm{WSS} - 2W\hat\pi(y, w)^2\right] + \hat\pi(y, w)^2 \\ &=& \frac{1}{W}\mathrm{WSS} - \hat\pi(y, w)^2. \end{eqnarray*}

where \(W = \sum_{i=1}^nw_i\) is the sum of weights (i.e. the number of samples in the unweighted case), \(\hat\pi(y, w) = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^nw_iy_i\) is the weighted prediction of the \(y_i\)'s (i.e. the average of the \(y_i\)'s in the unweighted case) and \(\mathrm{WSS} = \sum_{i=1}^nw_iy_i^2\) denotes the weighted sum of squares.

With this formulation, only \(\hat\pi(y, w)\) and \(\mathrm{WSS}\) need to be maintained at all time for the loss to be evaluated.
